# AI Research Digest — Saturday, May 09, 2026

## TL;DR
- **Anthropic published a sharp alignment training result.** "Teaching Claude Why" reports blackmail rates of **0% for Claude Haiku 4.5 and later, vs. up to 96% for Opus 4** — a 3M-token "difficult advice" set matched 85M tokens of synthetic honeypot data (28× efficiency). ## Items

### 1. Teaching Claude Why
- **Source:** Anthropic Research (May 8, 2026)
- **Link:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/teaching-claude-why
- **Authors / org:** Anthropic Alignment team
- **Focus area:** Frontier models / Alignment training methodology
- **What it is:** A safety-training write-up arguing that teaching the *principles* underlying aligned behavior generalizes better than demonstrations alone. Three concrete techniques: (a) a 3M-token **"Difficult Advice" dataset** where users (not Claude) face dilemmas and Claude gives constitution-aligned advice; (b) **constitutional documents** plus fictional narratives depicting aligned AI; (c) **diverse training environments** — varied system prompts and tool definitions on top of standard chat data. Headline numbers: **0% blackmail rate for all models from Haiku 4.5 onward** vs. up to **96% for Opus 4**; the 3M-token Difficult Advice set matched 85M tokens of synthetic honeypot data (**28× efficiency**); constitutional documents alone moved blackmail **65% → 19%** (>3× reduction); adding tool definitions and diverse system prompts to the training mix produced "noticeably faster improvement on honeypot evaluations."
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Two production takeaways. (1) The "diverse system prompts and tool definitions" finding is direct: agent products that train against narrow chat distributions over-fit to that distribution. If you fine-tune anything in your stack — even a small router or summarizer — vary the system prompts and tool schemas heavily during training. (2) The 28× data efficiency from "advice to a struggling user" framing vs. "Claude in a dilemma" framing is a clean recipe for safety SFT in companion-app settings: instead of role-playing the model into bad situations, role-play the user. Pairs with yesterday's Natural Language Autoencoders work — NLAs *measure* eval-awareness gaps, this paper attempts to *close* them.
- **Action signal:** `track` for the methodology; relates-to [anthropic-research-2026-05-07-natural-language-autoencoders] in the claims ledger (NLAs measure the gap; this paper closes it from the training side).

### 2. AI Co-Mathematician: Accelerating Mathematicians with Agentic AI
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.AI, May 8)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06651
- **Authors / org:** Daniel Zheng, Ingrid von Glehn, Yori Zwols, Lars Buesing, Martin Wattenberg, Pushmeet Kohli, et al. — **Google DeepMind** (per author affiliations)
- **Focus area:** Frontier models / Reasoning & inference-time compute / Agent orchestration
- **What it is:** A workbench for human–AI mathematical research with a **stateful, asynchronous workspace** that tracks hypotheses, manages uncertainty, and supports ideation, literature search, computational exploration, theorem proving, and theory building. Reported: **48% on FrontierMath Tier 4** (new high score among evaluated AI systems); in early human testing, helped researchers solve open problems and identify new directions.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** The interesting bit isn't the FrontierMath number — it's the **architecture**: an asynchronous, stateful workspace tracking *uncertainty* and *hypothesis evolution*, not just message history. That's the right primitive for any research-grade companion app (or coding agent on a multi-day task). Most current agent stacks treat state as "the chat thread plus a vector store"; this work treats state as a hypothesis graph that the human can branch and merge. Worth borrowing the data model even outside math.
- **Action signal:** `track` — no public release noted. Architectural pattern is portable today.

### 3. StraTA: Incentivizing Agentic RL with Strategic Trajectory Abstraction
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.CL, May 8)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06642
- **Authors / org:** Xiangyuan Xue, Yifan Zhou, Zidong Wang, Shengji Tang, Philip Torr, Wanli Ouyang, Lei Bai, Zhenfei Yin
- **Focus area:** Long-running / autonomous loops / Agent orchestration
- **What it is:** Hierarchical agent RL that **samples a compact strategy from the initial task state**, conditions all subsequent actions on it, and **jointly trains strategy generation and action execution** via GRPO-style hierarchical rollouts with diverse strategy sampling and self-judgment. Reported: **93.1% ALFWorld**, **84.2% WebShop**, **63.5% SciWorld** (claimed to outperform frontier closed-source models on SciWorld).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Concrete pattern for any planner-executor split: have the planner emit a **compact, conditioning strategy** at task start, and treat all subsequent actions as conditionals. This avoids the standard failure mode where the executor drifts from the plan because the plan is just text in context. The "self-judgment" mechanism (model rates its own strategy candidates during rollouts) is implementable today as a sampling-time pattern in agent harnesses, even without RL.
- **Action signal:** `compare` — direct comparison vs. yesterday's BEACON ([arxiv:2605.06078] **92.9% ALFWorld**). Two competing mechanisms — top-down strategy abstraction (StraTA) vs. bottom-up milestone-segmented reward shaping (BEACON) — converge to ~93% on the same benchmark. Flagged in claims ledger.

### 4. Beyond Semantic Similarity: Rethinking Retrieval for Agentic Search via Direct Corpus Interaction
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.CL/cs.IR, May 7) — HF Daily Papers top item, 37 votes
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05242
- **Authors / org:** Zhuofeng Li et al. (incl. Yejin Choi, Jiawei Han, Jimmy Lin)
- **Focus area:** Tools & connectors / Retrieval / Agent design
- **What it is:** Argues that compressing corpus access into a single top-k retrieval step bottlenecks agentic search. **Direct Corpus Interaction (DCI)** gives the agent **grep, file reads, and shell commands** over the raw corpus — no embeddings, no vector index, no retrieval API. Reported: "substantially outperforms strong sparse, dense, and reranking baselines on several BRIGHT and BEIR datasets" and strong results on BrowseComp-Plus and multi-hop QA without conventional retrievers.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Direct vindication of the "MCP-as-shell-access" approach for any agent that searches a local corpus (notes vault, codebase, document store). For Justin's Obsidian vault specifically, this implies: stop trying to embed everything; expose `ripgrep` as an MCP tool and let Claude drive search. Pairs with yesterday's RTriever-4B reasoning-aware reranker — the two are complementary, not competing: DCI replaces the *coarse* retrieval step, rerankers improve the *fine* one when you do have a candidate set.
- **Action signal:** `try-now` — implementable as an MCP server today. The architecture is simpler than the existing vector-store stack and may eliminate one moving part.

### 5. LatentRAG: Latent Reasoning and Retrieval for Efficient Agentic RAG
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.CL, May 8)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06285
- **Authors / org:** Yijia Zheng, Marcel Worring (University of Amsterdam)
- **Focus area:** Context engineering / Retrieval / Inference efficiency
- **What it is:** Moves agentic-RAG reasoning out of natural language and **into latent space**: thoughts and subqueries are emitted as latent tokens directly from hidden states in a single forward pass, rather than autoregressively generated as text. Aligns the LLM with the dense retriever in shared latent space; a parallel decoder recovers natural language for transparency. Reported: **~90% inference latency reduction** vs. explicit agentic RAG, with comparable accuracy across seven benchmarks.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** If the latency claim holds, this is the right shape for streaming, low-latency conversational agents (companion apps especially). The **transparency layer** matters too — by decoding latent tokens back to text on demand, you get audit trails without paying the autoregressive cost on the hot path. Today this is research-grade — the technique requires aligning the LLM and retriever in shared latent space, not a drop-in. But the architectural lesson is portable: the multi-hop "think → search → think" loop doesn't need to be text the whole way through.
- **Action signal:** `track` — promising but requires retraining the retriever-LLM pair. Watch for an open-weights instantiation.

### 6. PrefixGuard: From LLM-Agent Traces to Online Failure-Warning Monitors
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.AI, May 8)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06455
- **Authors / org:** Xinmiao Huang, Jinwei Hu, Rajarshi Roy, Changshun Wu, Yi Dong, Xiaowei Huang
- **Focus area:** Long-running / autonomous loops / Evaluation / Observability
- **What it is:** A monitor-synthesis framework for **detecting agent failures during execution**, not at terminal. Two phases: (a) **StepView induction** converts heterogeneous traces into deterministic, typed-step adapters; (b) **supervised monitor training** learns event abstractions and prefix-risk scoring from terminal outcomes, predicting failure on partial trajectories. Reported AUPRC: **WebArena 0.900, τ²-Bench 0.710, SkillsBench 0.533, TerminalBench 0.557**. Average **+0.137 AUPRC over raw-text baselines**; LLM-as-judge "underperformed significantly using the same protocol."
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the missing piece between "I have logs" and "I have a heartbeat that wakes me up." For any long-running autonomous loop, a per-prefix risk score lets you abort or hand off mid-trajectory rather than waiting for terminal failure. The `WebArena 0.900 AUPRC` matters: an early-warning monitor with that precision-recall is good enough to gate destructive actions in a real product. The negative result on LLM-as-judge is also operationally useful — don't try to use a model to monitor a model in this regime.
- **Action signal:** `try-now` for the supervised-monitor pattern (logs → typed steps → prefix risk score). Pairs cleanly with [arxiv:2605.04785] AgentTrust from May 7 (runtime tool-call interception); PrefixGuard is the trajectory-level analogue to AgentTrust's call-level interception.

### 7. Can RL Teach Long-Horizon Reasoning to LLMs? Expressiveness Is Key
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.CL, May 8)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06638
- **Authors / org:** Tianle Wang, Zhaoyang Wang, Guangchen Lan, Xinpeng Wei, Sipeng Zhang, Guanwen Qiu, Abulhair Saparov
- **Focus area:** Reasoning & inference-time compute / RL training methodology
- **What it is:** Introduces **ScaleLogic**, a controlled synthetic framework that decouples reasoning *depth* (proof length) from *expressiveness* (logical complexity — implication, conjunction, disjunction, negation, universal quantification). Headline: RL training compute scales as a power law in depth (**T ∝ D^γ, R² > 0.99**), and the exponent **γ rises monotonically from 1.04 to 2.60** as expressiveness increases. Training on richer logical structures yields downstream gains **up to +10.66 points** with better transfer.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Methodologically important for anyone doing RL on agent traces. The takeaway is *what* you train on dominates *how much* you train — synthetic agentic tasks at low expressiveness will not transfer to real-world reasoning even with more compute. For Justin: when designing synthetic agent training data (e.g., for an MCP-aware fine-tune), include explicit negation, quantification, and disjunction — not just deeper trajectories of simple tool calls. Interacts with the BEACON / StraTA / AEM line: those papers improve credit assignment; this one says the underlying logical complexity of the task ceiling matters more.
- **Action signal:** `track` for design intuition when synthesizing agent training data.

### 8. EMO: Pretraining Mixture of Experts for Emergent Modularity
- **Source:** Allen AI Blog + HuggingFace (May 8) + arXiv [2605.06663]
- **Link:** https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/emo
- **Authors / org:** Allen Institute for AI (Ryan Wang, Akshita Bhagia, Sewon Min, et al.)
- **Focus area:** Local & open models for Apple Silicon / Frontier-adjacent / MoE architecture
- **What it is:** A **1B-active / 14B-total MoE** (8 active experts of 128) trained on 1T tokens with **document-level expert pooling**: every token in a document routes through a shared subset of experts, sampled by averaging router preferences across the document. Pool size is randomized during training to support varying inference-time subset sizes. Result: experts specialize on **semantic domains** (Health, US Politics, Film & Music, etc.) rather than the surface features standard MoEs cluster on. Headline trade-off: at **12.5% of experts (16/128) → ~3% absolute accuracy drop**; at **25% (32/128) → ~1% drop**. Standard MoEs collapse to near-random in the same selective regime.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Two things. (1) **Local-fit**: a 14B MoE that runs at near-full quality on a fraction of total weights is exactly the right shape for M5 Max — you can keep all 128 experts memory-resident with substantial KV/context headroom, and selectively activate domain-specific subsets per session (legal/medical/code/etc.). (2) **Interpretability**: domain-clustered experts mean you can route a session deterministically based on detected topic, getting some of the benefits of separate fine-tuned models without maintaining them.
- **Action signal:** `local-fit` + `try-now` — weights, code, and visualization released today (HF collection, GitHub `allenai/EMO`).

## Local-model corner
**EMO** (Allen AI, released May 8) is the local-relevant item today.

- **Model:** EMO — 14B total parameters, 1B active per token, 128 experts (8 active), 1T training tokens. Released under public license (HF + GitHub `allenai/EMO`).
- **Memory fit on M5 Max (64 GB):**
  - Full 14B at **bf16 ≈ 28 GB** weights → fits with plenty of headroom for KV cache. Plausible 64K+ context with active 1B experts.
  - **Q4 ≈ 7 GB** weights → leaves ~50 GB+ for very long context (200K+ on a single document) or background services.
  - The selective-expert mode (12.5% / 25%) is most useful when you want to keep many domain-specialized experts loaded and route per-session — *all* 128 experts at Q4 is still ~7 GB, well under budget.
- **Realistic tasks:** General chat, RAG over the Obsidian vault, light coding, classification/routing, structured extraction. **Not** a reasoning frontier — at 1B active parameters per token, this won't replace Opus 4.6 / Sonnet 4.6 for long-horizon planning or hard math. The right framing is "fast local 1B with 14B's breadth," not "small frontier model."
- **How to run:** Initially **HuggingFace Transformers** (released today). MLX port likely within days given Allen AI's track record; llama.cpp/GGUF will follow. For interactive use today on M5 Max, use `transformers` with MPS backend or wait 1–2 weeks for an MLX build.
- **Speed estimate:** No M5 Max-specific benchmark yet. By analogy to other 1B-active MoEs at bf16 on M5 Max (~600 GB/s memory bandwidth), expect **80–140 tok/s** uncached single-stream for the active 1B path. Q4 should push toward 200+ tok/s. Treat as rough until reproduced.

## Conflicts & open questions

- **StraTA 93.1% ALFWorld vs. BEACON 92.9% ALFWorld** — Two papers from May 7–8 reach nearly identical SOTA on the same benchmark via mechanistically distinct training-time fixes: BEACON ([arxiv:2605.06078]) uses milestone-segmented temporal reward shaping; StraTA ([arxiv:2605.06642]) uses top-down strategy abstraction with hierarchical GRPO. Both also report WebShop and SciWorld but with different splits. Open question: are these complementary (run both)? Or does one ceiling-out the other? Worth tracking whether anyone composes them in the next 2–4 weeks.
- **DCI vs. RTriever-4B (BRIGHT/BEIR)** — May 7's [arxiv:2605.04018] argued for *better* reranking on a reasoning-aware retrieval benchmark; today's DCI ([arxiv:2605.05242]) argues for *no retrieval* — give the agent grep. Both report wins on BRIGHT. They aren't strict contradictions (different retrieval-stage targets), but they pull in opposite architectural directions: more learned retrieval vs. less. The interesting reproduction is whether DCI + RTriever-4B together beats either alone.
- **Open empirical question (no contradiction yet):** LatentRAG's claimed 90% latency reduction comes without a head-to-head comparison against the strongest text-RAG baselines on equivalent compute. Watch for an independent reproduction.
