# AI Research Digest — Friday, June 19, 2026

## TL;DR
The June 18 arXiv batch lands a **DeepSeek frontier model release** plus a **fourth per-user-memory substrate in five days** and the **cleanest KV-cache-tier RAG cost win to date** — three items at the top of the digest are operationally relevant. **DeepSeek-V4 ([arxiv:2606.19348])** ships an MoE pair (V4-Pro 1.6T/49B activated; V4-Flash 284B/13B activated) at **1M-token context with KV cache cut to 10% of V3.2** via hybrid attention (Compressed Sparse Attention + Heavily Compressed Attention) and **27% of single-token inference FLOPs**, trained on 32T+ tokens — the strongest open million-token claim in 2026. **AtomMem ([arxiv:2606.19847])** extracts atomic facts → hierarchical events → temporal profiles → associative memory graph; **state-of-the-art on LoCoMo** with a much simpler substrate than the Bojie Li programs covered this week. **CacheWeaver ([arxiv:2606.19667])** drops **median TTFT 20–33% on grounded RAG** via prefix-tree reordering of retrieved evidence — a prompt-layer fix that works on stock vLLM, reaching **97.5% of oracle ordering's gain**. Anthropic's Frontier Red Team also dropped **Project Fetch: Phase Two** (June 18) — **Claude Opus 4.7 controls a robotic quadruped ~20× faster than the fastest human team** and **37.7× faster than non-Claude humans** on four shared tasks, with 1,045 LOC vs 10,309 for the human-Claude team.

- **DeepSeek-V4 ([arxiv:2606.19348]) is the strongest 1M-context open-model claim this year.** 1.6T MoE / 49B activated for V4-Pro + 284B MoE / 13B activated for V4-Flash, **KV cache 10% of V3.2 at 1M tokens**, **single-token inference FLOPs cut to 27%**, three architectural primitives (CSA + HCA hybrid attention, Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections, Muon optimizer), 32T+ pretraining tokens. Weights on Hugging Face under `deepseek-ai/deepseek-v4`. This is the cleanest single-paper push on the long-context KV-cache axis since the V3.2 release.
- **AtomMem ([arxiv:2606.19847]) is the fourth distinct per-user-memory substrate this week and the simplest.** Four-stage pipeline: atomic-fact extraction → hierarchical event structures → temporal profiles → associative memory graph at retrieval. **State-of-the-art on LoCoMo across reasoning tasks**. The architectural read against the Bojie Li three-paper program ([arxiv:2606.16707], [arxiv:2606.17929], [arxiv:2606.19172]) is **direct**: AtomMem stays in the context tier (no per-user weights, no compiled code), but adds the **hierarchical-event + temporal-profile layer** that UaC's typed-code substrate doesn't model explicitly.
- **CacheWeaver ([arxiv:2606.19667]) is the cleanest single-paper KV-cache win for grounded RAG since prefix caching shipped in vLLM.** Greedy walk over a prefix tree of recently served evidence sequences places the **most reusable prefix first**, so set-overlap actually becomes prefix-overlap. **TTFT down 20–33% across three vLLM configs at no quality cost**; the greedy policy reaches **97.5% of oracle ordering's gain**, which is the load-bearing number — most reusable prefix locality is recoverable by a prompt-layer scheduler without changing the serving engine.
- **HydraHead ([arxiv:2606.20097]) hybridizes Full Attention and Linear Attention at the head axis instead of the layer axis** — and the empirical case for that move is **+69% over baseline at 512K context** on only 15B training tokens, **matching a 3:1 layer-wise hybrid's long-context performance at a 7:1 LA-to-FA ratio**. Interpretability analysis identifies retrieval-critical heads; FA is preserved only for them. The architecture is what every hybrid open-weight model should be benchmarking against next.
- **Anthropic — *Project Fetch: Phase Two* (June 18, Ilie / Freeman / Troy) lands the strongest single-model robotics result Anthropic has published.** Claude Opus 4.7 autonomously controlled a robotic quadruped — connecting to sensors, writing control programs, detecting objects — **~20× faster than the fastest human team**, **37.7× faster than non-Claude humans on four shared tasks**, **18.9× faster than Claude-assisted humans**, **1,045 LOC vs 10,309 for the human-Claude team for equal-or-better results**. Anthropic explicitly disclaims the "LLMs have solved robotics" framing — closed-loop control (autonomous beach-ball retrieval) still breaks — but the result is the **first frontier-model robotics win that doesn't go through behavior cloning or a vision-language-action model**.
- **Autonomous Event-Driven Multi-Agent Orchestration ([arxiv:2606.20058], Dhanyamraju / Raghav / Lee) is the first paper to quantify the orchestration-at-enterprise-scale ceiling.** 208 production-derived scenarios across **Persona (<10 agents) / Department (20–80) / Enterprise (200)** scales: at enterprise scale, **agent discovery noise becomes the primary bottleneck, and simple tasks degrade more sharply than complex ones** (the surprising direction). DAG Plan-and-Execute is precise at small scale but overhead-bound at enterprise scale; ReAct degrades more gracefully. Their Task Manager (priority inference + related-event merging + preemption) **cuts high-priority queue latency 14–75% and improves related-event correctness >20 percentage points at enterprise scale**.
- **SAGE-OPD ([arxiv:2606.19659], Zhou et al.) is the cleanest multi-turn on-policy distillation result for agent training.** Verifier-free selective intervention — observe environment feedback first, decide whether to skip or intervene per turn, weight by teacher confidence on off-distribution histories. **Up to +13.3% relative improvement in ALFWorld unseen success rate over standard OPD**. The selective-intervention primitive is the right answer to the compounding-error problem in multi-turn agent training.
- **Beyond Static Leaderboards ([arxiv:2606.19704], Patel et al., IBM, 61 authors) is the methodological successor to SEAGym.** Argues aggregate-score leaderboards systematically underspecify deployed-agent performance, introduces **predictive validity (correlation between in-sample and out-of-distribution rank)** as the right metric, and proposes a **twelve-tier measurement framework** plus three falsifiable OOD criteria. Fourteen parallel implementation studies on an MCP-based industrial agent benchmark — the largest coordinated evaluation-methodology study to date.

### 1. DeepSeek-V4 — 1.6T / 284B MoE pair, 1M-token context, KV cache cut to 10% of V3.2
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348
- **Authors / org:** DeepSeek-AI (319 authors total; submitted by Wenfeng Liang, April 26 preprint date but indexed June 18)
- **Focus area:** Frontier models + context engineering
- **What it is:** Preview release of two MoE models — **V4-Pro (1.6T parameters, 49B activated)** and **V4-Flash (284B parameters, 13B activated)** — both supporting **1M-token context**. Three architectural innovations: (1) **hybrid attention combining Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA) and Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA)** — the KV-cache axis; (2) **Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections** for stable residual flow at depth; (3) **Muon optimizer** for training stability. Pretrained on **32T+ tokens** with full post-training. **Reported numbers** at 1M-token context vs V3.2: **27% of single-token inference FLOPs, 10% of KV cache**. Weights on Hugging Face: `deepseek-ai/deepseek-v4`.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Three reads. (a) The **10% KV-cache claim at 1M tokens** is the largest single-paper move on the long-context cost axis in 2026 and matters even when Justin doesn't run V4-class weights — the CSA/HCA hybrid is a template the open ecosystem will copy into 30–70B class models within weeks. (b) **V4-Flash at 13B activated** is the more operationally relevant claim: a 284B-parameter MoE that only activates 13B per token is **a different cost regime than dense 70B**, and the 13B activated path is the right benchmark for the next-generation hosted-frontier vs local-frontier tradeoff. (c) The **1M context window** at this cost profile changes the calculus for **document-grounded coding agents** — a full federal-data corpus fits in context, and the cost-per-query falls into the range where Justin's MCP-tool products can afford to **stop chunking large code repos** and just stream the relevant subtree. The architecture is the headline; the cost numbers are the actionable signal.
- **Action signal:** `track` — V4-Pro weights are released but cluster-scale inference puts this out of reach for indie deployment today; the architecture is the durable read. Also `compare` — direct numerical counter to the "long-context is always expensive" prior; the 10% KV-cache claim should be cross-checked against any future open-model long-context benchmark.

### 2. AtomMem — atomic-fact memory + hierarchical events + temporal profiles + associative graph; LoCoMo SOTA
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19847
- **Authors / org:** Yanyu Yao, Shangze Li, Zhi Zheng, Hui Zheng, Qi Liu, Tong Xu, Enhong Chen (USTC + collaborators); 19 pages, 10 figures, 5 tables
- **Focus area:** Agent memory
- **What it is:** Argues prior memory-augmented agent systems build memory **coarsely** (raw turns, untyped summaries) and **unstably** (unconstrained updates). AtomMem's four-stage pipeline: (1) a **Fact Executor** that selectively extracts **high-value atomic facts** from long-form interactions; (2) **hierarchical event structures** organizing facts into coherent episodic contexts; (3) **temporal profiles** that track dynamically evolving user attributes over time (drift / preference change / context shift); (4) an **associative memory graph** activated at retrieval to connect fragmented memories. **Reported result**: **state-of-the-art on the LoCoMo benchmark** across reasoning tasks.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the **fourth distinct per-user-memory substrate covered this week**, and the cleanest architectural counter to the Bojie Li compile-to-substrate program. Three reads. (a) AtomMem stays in the **context tier** (no per-user weights, no compiled code) but adds the **hierarchical-event + temporal-profile layer** that UaC ([arxiv:2606.16707]) collapses into typed code and Engram ([arxiv:2606.19172]) collapses into parametric hash-keyed edits. The substrate question — *typed code vs parametric edits vs atomic-fact graph* — now has three sharp data points within five days. (b) The **temporal-profile layer** is the operationally novel piece: most companion-app memory stacks (mem0, LangMem, Letta) store facts as immutable triples; AtomMem treats user attributes as **time-indexed and explicitly drift-aware**, which is the right model for a personal-agent product where preferences and context churn weekly. (c) The **associative-graph retrieval** matters for Justin's federal-data side — atomic facts extracted from RFPs / compliance documents could connect across document boundaries without re-embedding the whole corpus.
- **Action signal:** `try-now` — atomic-fact extraction + hierarchical events + associative-graph retrieval is implementable on top of any existing companion-app stack without model changes (no code release noted yet, but the recipe is fully described). Also `compare` — direct contrast with **UaC's typed-code substrate** ([arxiv:2606.16707]) and **Engram's parametric-edit substrate** ([arxiv:2606.19172]). The open empirical question — *which substrate dominates at which user-content size and which reasoning regime?* — is now the most important open thread in this cluster.

### 3. CacheWeaver — prefix-tree evidence ordering for grounded RAG; TTFT −20–33%, 97.5% of oracle
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19667
- **Authors / org:** Kaizhen Tan, Rong Gu, Mingyuan Li
- **Focus area:** Context engineering + tools & connectors
- **What it is:** Diagnoses why **prefix caching in serving engines like vLLM underperforms in grounded RAG**: adjacent queries retrieve **overlapping evidence in different orders**, so the *set* overlap doesn't translate to *prefix* overlap that the cache can exploit. CacheWeaver is a **prompt-layer scheduler** (engine-untouched, retrieval-untouched) that maintains a **prefix tree over recently served evidence sequences** and does a **greedy walk to place the most reusable prefix first**. **Reported numbers**: across three vLLM configurations, **median TTFT down 20–33%** vs retrieval-order prefix caching, with no measured drop in answer quality on QA evaluations. The headline result: the greedy policy reaches **97.5% of the median TTFT gain from oracle ordering**.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the **highest-leverage drop-in for any RAG-backed MCP-tool product Justin runs**. Three reads. (a) The **20–33% TTFT cut on stock vLLM at no engineering cost** is the kind of latency win that doesn't usually exist without rewriting the serving engine — CacheWeaver lives entirely above the engine, as a prompt-layer reordering pass between retrieval and inference. (b) The **97.5%-of-oracle headline** is the load-bearing claim: it says **most of the reusable prefix locality is recoverable by simple scheduling**, which means the cost-benefit calc isn't "build a complex cache-aware retrieval system" — it's "add a prefix-tree pass before the prompt assembles." That's the right kind of result for an indie stack. (c) Pairs structurally with **DSG / Decoupled Search Grounding** from June 17 ([arxiv:2606.18947]) — DSG moves grounding to an MCP gateway with cache as a first-class control; CacheWeaver gives the gateway the **right ordering policy**. Together they're the cleanest two-paper grounded-RAG production stack of the month.
- **Action signal:** `try-now` — implementable as a Python-layer pass on any existing vLLM deployment; no code release noted but the algorithm (prefix tree + greedy walk) is one weekend. Also `local-fit` — works on Justin's M5 Max stack as soon as he switches to a vLLM-compatible runtime (e.g., MLC-LLM with prefix caching).

### 4. HydraHead — head-axis FA / LA attention hybridization, +69% over baseline at 512K context on 15B training tokens
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20097
- **Authors / org:** Zhentao Tan, Wei Chen, Jingyi Shen, Yao Liu, Xu Shen, Yue Wu, Jieping Ye
- **Focus area:** Context engineering + frontier-model architecture
- **What it is:** Most open-source hybrid attention models do **layer-wise** mixing (some layers use Full Attention, others Linear Attention). HydraHead argues this misses the **finer-grained heterogeneity within a layer**: interpretability analysis shows that **layers exhibit block-wise functional similarity, but individual heads within the same layer display distinct functional specialization**. The architecture hybridizes FA and LA **along the head axis** with two innovations: (1) an **interpretability-driven selection strategy** identifies retrieval-critical heads and **preserves FA only for them**; (2) a **scale-normalized fusion module** reconciles distributional gaps between FA and LA outputs. **Reported numbers**: trained on only **15B tokens** via three-stage transfer with parameter reuse and distillation. **+69% over baseline at 512K context length**, approaching **Qwen3.5 (256K native context) at comparable parameter size**; matches a **3:1 layer-wise hybrid's long-context performance at a 7:1 LA-to-FA ratio**.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Three reads. (a) The **7:1 LA-to-FA ratio matching 3:1 layer-wise** result is the architectural argument for head-level hybridization as the **next-generation default for long-context open models** — the per-token attention cost drops further than any layer-wise hybrid can reach. (b) **Trained on only 15B tokens** with a transfer-pipeline approach is operationally important — head-axis hybridization is **cheap to retrofit onto existing pretrained models** rather than requiring a from-scratch run. This is the kind of result that should ship inside open-weight 30–70B models within months. (c) The **+69% at 512K** crossover is the right benchmark for the long-document reasoning regime Justin's federal-data work runs in — once 30–70B-class open models adopt this, the 256K → 512K barrier disappears at Linear-Attention-class cost.
- **Action signal:** `track` — architecture-only result (no model weights or code release noted); the durable read is the **head-level hybridization template** that other model families will adopt. Also `compare` — direct counter to the "layer-wise is the right granularity for hybrid attention" prior.

### 5. Anthropic — Project Fetch: Phase Two; Claude Opus 4.7 controls a robotic quadruped ~20× faster than the fastest human team
- **Source:** Anthropic Research
- **Link:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-fetch-phase-two
- **Authors / org:** Michael Ilie, C. Daniel Freeman, Kevin K. Troy (Anthropic Frontier Red Team), June 18, 2026
- **Focus area:** Frontier models + tools & connectors (robotics agent control)
- **What it is:** Updated Frontier Red Team experiment from 2025's original Project Fetch — can a frontier model **autonomously control a robotic quadruped** through code? Three trials with Claude Opus 4.7 in Claude Code with maximum adaptive thinking; human involvement limited to **laptop setup, initial prompting, task approvals**. Tasks include connecting to sensors, writing control programs, object detection. **Reported numbers**: Claude Opus 4.7 was **~20× faster than the fastest human team**; on four shared tasks Opus 4.7 averaged **37.7× faster than non-Claude humans** and **18.9× faster than Claude-assisted humans**; generated **1,045 LOC vs 10,309 LOC for the human-Claude team** at equal-or-better results, with **minimal variance across three trial runs**. Anthropic explicitly notes the model **struggles with precise closed-loop control** (autonomous beach-ball retrieval still breaks) and disclaims any "LLMs have solved robotics" framing.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Three reads. (a) The **20× / 37.7× / 18.9× cluster** is the first frontier-model robotics result that **doesn't go through a vision-language-action model or behavior cloning** — Claude Opus 4.7 writes control code directly, executes it on hardware, and observes outcomes through the same MCP-tool surface a coding agent uses. This is the **clean architectural argument that "robotics is a coding-agent problem"** for a wider range of tasks than previously believed. (b) The **1,045 vs 10,309 LOC** delta is the more operationally important number — it says the agent is **converging on tight code that does the right thing**, not generating bloat that happens to work. That's the property Justin's MCP-tool products need. (c) The **closed-loop limitation** is the right operational read: for genuinely real-time control (vision-driven motion control, dynamic balance), the code-then-execute loop still loses. The dividing line — discrete-task code vs continuous-control feedback — is the actionable framing.
- **Action signal:** `track` — Anthropic-internal experiment, not a product; the durable read is the **"code-then-execute" robotics architecture** as a generalizable agent pattern beyond robotics (any tool-stack where the agent writes the control code and the tool executes it). Also `compare` — direct numerical counter to the "behavior cloning / VLA dominates for embodied agents" prior, with the closed-loop caveat as the proper qualification.

### 6. Autonomous Event-Driven Multi-Agent Orchestration — 208 enterprise scenarios; scale (not complexity) dominates
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20058
- **Authors / org:** Harsh Rao Dhanyamraju, Leonidas Raghav, Aaron Lee
- **Focus area:** Agent orchestration + long-running / autonomous loops
- **What it is:** Most multi-agent benchmarks (CrewAI, AutoGen, LangGraph case studies) assume **discrete request-response workflows**. This paper evaluates two architectures (**DAG Plan-and-Execute** and **ReAct**) under **continuous event-monitoring conditions** across **208 production-derived enterprise scenarios** at three scales: **Persona (<10 agents) / Department (20–80) / Enterprise (200)**. Introduces a **Task Manager** for continuous operation via **priority inference + related-event merging + preemption**. **Reported findings**: (1) **scale dominates orchestration performance, not task complexity** — both architectures perform well at small scale but degrade at enterprise scale as **agent discovery noise becomes the primary bottleneck**; (2) the **counterintuitive direction** — simple tasks degrade more sharply than complex ones (orchestration overhead is fixed per task, so simple tasks are proportionally penalized more); (3) DAG Plan-and-Execute is precise + structured at small scale but overhead-worsened at enterprise scale; ReAct degrades more gracefully via incremental failure handling. **Task Manager numbers**: **high-priority queue latency cut 14–75%** at enterprise scale; **related-event correctness improved >20 percentage points**.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Three reads. (a) The **scale-not-complexity finding** is the **single most important orchestration-tier framing this year** — the operational implication is that **the right axis to optimize for is agent discovery, not per-task planning quality**. For Justin's MCP-tool products, this argues for **investing in tool / agent discovery infrastructure (registries, semantic indexing, capability cards) before investing in better per-task planning** at the architectural level. (b) The **ReAct-degrades-gracefully** result is direct counter to the conventional wisdom that **structured DAG planning beats incremental ReAct at scale** — at enterprise scale, the DAG's higher overhead dominates. This shifts the architecture-selection decision for any orchestrator Justin builds. (c) The Task Manager's **priority inference + related-event merging + preemption triad** is the right primitive set for **long-running autonomous loops** — Justin's heartbeat-driven companion-app architectures should adopt these three first-class.
- **Action signal:** `track` — closed-source enterprise study with no code; the durable read is the **scale-not-complexity framing** and the **Task Manager primitive set**. Also `compare` — direct counter to the "DAG planning beats ReAct at scale" prior; the enterprise-scale crossover point is the new empirical claim.

### 7. SAGE-OPD — selective agent-guided intervention for multi-turn on-policy distillation; +13.3% ALFWorld
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19659
- **Authors / org:** Yuhang Zhou, Lizhu Zhang, Yifan Wu, Mingyi Wang, Bo Peng, Jiayi Liu, Xiangjun Fan, Zhuokai Zhao
- **Focus area:** Agent training + agent orchestration
- **What it is:** Standard on-policy distillation (OPD) trains the student on its own policy trajectories — promising for fixing exposure bias but **brittle in multi-turn** settings, where (1) early errors compound across the trajectory, (2) dense token-level OPD **over-penalizes semantically valid alternatives**, (3) **repeated-action local degeneracies** get reinforced, (4) teacher supervision becomes **unreliable on off-distribution histories**. SAGE-OPD addresses all four with a **verifier-free selective intervention** scheme: (a) observe environment feedback first, (b) use teacher judgment to **decide per-turn whether to skip or intervene** on the student's response, (c) **weight token-level distillation by teacher confidence** so uncertain teacher distributions on corrupted histories have less influence, (d) apply **loss normalization** to preserve standard-OPD's loss scale. **Reported result**: **up to +13.3% relative improvement in ALFWorld unseen success rate over standard OPD**.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Two reads. (a) The **selective-intervention primitive** is the right answer to the compounding-error problem in any multi-turn agent training loop — Justin's MCP-tool agents trained with imitation-from-Claude pipelines will run into the same brittleness, and the **observe-feedback-first then decide-to-intervene** pattern transfers directly. (b) The **teacher-confidence weighting on off-distribution histories** is the operationally novel bit: most multi-turn training pipelines treat teacher supervision as a constant, which is wrong when the student has wandered into a state the teacher wasn't trained on. The **verifier-free** framing matters — Justin doesn't have to spin up a separate reward model or step verifier to use this; teacher confidence is already accessible. Pairs structurally with **RODS** ([arxiv:2606.19047], June 17) — RODS attacks the **data-side bottleneck**, SAGE-OPD attacks the **supervision-side bottleneck** in the same loop.
- **Action signal:** `track` for the architecture; `try-now` for the **observe-then-intervene + confidence-weighted-distillation** primitives, which are implementable in any existing multi-turn agent training pipeline without changes to the teacher or environment. Also `compare` — strengthens the broader cluster claim that **multi-turn agent training requires fundamentally different supervision than single-turn**.

### 8. Beyond Static Leaderboards — predictive validity as the right eval metric for LLM agents; 14 parallel studies
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19704
- **Authors / org:** Dhaval C. Patel, Kaoutar El Maghraoui, Shuxin Lin et al. (IBM, **61 authors total**)
- **Focus area:** Evaluation
- **What it is:** Methodological argument that **aggregate-score leaderboards (HELM and successors) systematically underspecify deployed-agent evaluation**. The thesis: rankings from aggregate scores **fail to transfer to out-of-distribution settings**, with competition retrospectives showing **rank instability**. Three contributions: (1) a methodological shift from in-sample mean scores to **predictive validity — the correlation between in-sample and out-of-distribution rank**; (2) a **twelve-tier measurement framework** that exposes deployment-relevant dimensions current frameworks collapse; (3) **three falsifiable out-of-distribution criteria** plus a pre-registered pilot design. Backed by **fourteen parallel implementation studies** on an MCP-based industrial agent benchmark, varying asset classes, orchestrations, retrieval strategies, reasoning modes, and infrastructure optimizations. The largest coordinated evaluation-methodology study published this year.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Two reads. (a) The **predictive-validity framing** is the **right metric** for any agent-leaderboard story Justin reads from now on — the question stops being "which model scores highest on benchmark X" and starts being "does benchmark X's ranking predict deployed performance on workload Y." For an indie product where the **deployed workload is narrow and idiosyncratic**, this framing is operationally critical. (b) The **twelve-tier measurement framework** is the methodological cousin to **SEAGym** ([arxiv:2606.17546], June 17) — both argue that aggregate scores hide critical dimensions, but where SEAGym focused on **multi-view evaluation of harness improvements**, this paper focuses on **predictive validity across deployment configurations**. Together they're the strongest twin-paper case in 2026 that **the next-generation agent benchmark needs to be multi-dimensional and OOD-validated, not single-score**.
- **Action signal:** `track` — methodological paper; the durable read is the **predictive-validity framing** as a critique-tool when reading future agent benchmarks. Also `compare` — strengthens the SEAGym cluster claim that **frequent leaderboard updates do not necessarily improve held-out performance**.
