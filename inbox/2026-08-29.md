# AI Research Digest — Saturday, August 29, 2026

## TL;DR
- **HarnessLens** [arxiv:2608.27311] is the missing *verification budget* layer on yesterday's harness-evolution pile (WikiSkill / PILOT / JIT-Agent). Propose-and-verify that scores every candidate on a fixed task set wastes rollouts and hides regressions. HarnessLens derives patches from traces and verifies only on behavior-relevant tasks via an attributable-evidence gate. Across three harnesses × four benchmarks: **+7.6–13.6%** held-out, less eval budget. Code: github.com/jhxu5214/HarnessLens. The weekend primitive is "don't gate a SKILL.md rewrite on the whole personal eval — gate it on the behaviors it claims to touch."
- **LoopHarness / Safety Does Not Compose** [arxiv:2608.27141] is the long-running-loop counterpart to yesterday's SARA. Trajectory-scoped monitors *re-initialize safety state* when the next episode starts. Against an attack whose evidence is fragmented across iterations, **every trajectory-scoped monitor has TPR = FPR**, no matter how expressive; a monitor that keeps cross-iteration state separates perfectly. Geometric decay of a risk score is not enough — the cooling-off a patient adversary waits is a **constant that does not grow with horizon N**. LoopHarness restores a persistent, non-decaying loop-level safety state and bounds expected unauthorized irreversible actions by **B+m−1+m/δ_M** (constant in N; the B+m−1 term is model-free and survives a colluding verifier).
- **ASIL** [arxiv:2608.26991] is the application-layer restatement of StateAct: screenshot-and-click is state-incomplete and brittle. Structured JSON observations + code-executable semantic actions, deepest feasible access path per app, 15 applications, 300 single-app + 80 multi-app tasks. Closed models **>80** success at **<5 actions/task**; repaired screenshot-and-click under a 50-step budget: **6.6 / 26.6** strict (15.0 / 53.3 on an easier OSWorld-comparable band). Beats LibreOffice UNO **+28–38** strict points; **only matches draw.io's MCP content contract**. Local-fit: Qwen3.5-2B SFT **58.0 → 72.1**, 9B **66.6 → 80.4**; light on-policy RL → **74.4 / 82.2**. Project: sharryxr.github.io/ASIL.
- **Zero-Shot Self-Orchestration** [arxiv:2608.26480] isolates the manager–worker scaffold over a shared filesystem — **no training, no per-benchmark tuning** — vs the same model in one pass. 100 latest hard LiveCodeBench, nine models. Gains are **real but conditional**: Qwen3.8-27B **+23.4**, GPT-5.6-Luna **+10.6**, Terra **+8.0** (five paired passes); Kimi-K3 **+30.4** / MiniMax-M3 **+11.0** with reasoning off (p < 10⁻⁴); Qwen3.6-35B **−1 to −9**. Opus-5 + manager: **91%** (study high) in one pass. Manager ~**3× tokens**, but Terra+manager **85.0 vs Fable 5 single-call 87.4** (p=0.59) at **$11.71 vs $61.11** per 100-problem pass; Qwen-27B arm **$51.75** on self-hostable weights. Recurring mechanisms: short worker calls + shared notes (context management) and decomposition.
- **Matched Trajectory Replay** [arxiv:2608.26846] (Prateek Chhikara) is the retrieve/answer/defer sibling of yesterday's Calibrated Enough. Holds answer states, evidence, budgets, and action costs fixed, then swaps raw verbalized confidence for post-hoc isotonic calibration. At the **same numerical commitment threshold**, calibration **changes which questions get answered**. Committed-answer accuracy **+up to 41pp** across six model×dataset pairs; coverage drops and retrieval rises. Overall **+up to 15pp** HotpotQA, **−up to 17pp** MuSiQue — a more selective operating point, not better answers. A pre-retrieval calibration map holds through retrieval depths 1–2 and **loses to raw confidence at depth 3**. Calibration is not a value-of-information estimator; retrieval needs its own VoI/utility head.

### 1. Verify Smarter, Evolve Further: HarnessLens
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27311
- **Authors / org:** Jinghan Xu / Yikai Zhang / Aili Chen / Weiyuan Li / Jiaqing Liang / Deqing Yang
- **Focus area:** Harness evolution + verification budget
- **What it is:** Automated harness evolution is now a pile (WikiSkill, PILOT, JIT-Agent, AutoSaddler). This paper attacks the *eval* that those loops hide behind. Default propose-and-verify scores every candidate modification on a fixed task set — expensive, and an aggregate score can hide a regression on the behavior you just patched. HarnessLens jointly explores the task space and user-configurable components, derives candidate modifications from execution trajectories, and selectively verifies each candidate on **behavior-relevant** tasks behind an **attributable-evidence gate**. Three agent harnesses, four benchmarks: **+7.6–13.6%** average held-out vs competing baselines, substantially less evaluation budget. Code: https://github.com/jhxu5214/HarnessLens. Abs verified 2026-08-29 (submitted 27 Aug 2026).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Yesterday's WikiSkill recipe was "gate skill writes on a small held-out set." HarnessLens says even that is the wrong *shape* of held-out set: score the behaviors the patch claims to change, and require attributable evidence, or you will ship a CLAUDE.md / SKILL.md edit that lifted the average and broke the thing you actually run. Concrete weekend experiment: when a maintainer pass proposes a skill patch, run only the 3–5 personal tasks that exercise that skill (WorkBuddy-style), not the whole 15-task mix, and refuse the patch if the targeted cell doesn't move. Do not train anything.
- **Action signal:** `try-now` on the targeted-eval gate; `compare` against WikiSkill's full-val-set gate and JIT-Agent's archive-of-harnesses before building a proposer.

### 2. Safety Does Not Compose: LoopHarness
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27141
- **Authors / org:** Chenhao Wu / Haoxuan Jia / Yang Liu / Yingguang Yang / Yuhan Lin / Chongyang Zhang / Hao Zheng / Yulin Huang / Jianshen Zhang / Yongzhi Qi / Shang Luo / Kefu Xu / Jifeng Zhu / Bin Chong
- **Focus area:** Long-running / autonomous loops + agent security
- **What it is:** Names a composition failure of the safeguards everyone actually ships. Agents now run as unattended loops (discover → plan → tool → verify → persist) from one human goal. Widely used monitors are **trajectory-scoped**: their safety state resets when the next trajectory begins. Central separation: against an attack whose evidence is fragmented across several iterations, **every trajectory-scoped monitor has true-positive rate equal to false-positive rate**, however expressive, because the evidence never co-occurs in the window it sees; a monitor that retains cross-iteration state separates the two perfectly. The obvious repair — a geometrically decaying risk score carried across episodes — fails: the cooling-off a patient adversary must wait is a **constant independent of horizon N**. LoopHarness restores a **persistent, non-decaying** safety state at the loop level. Under mediated commits and an arbiter detection floor δ_M, expected unauthorized irreversible actions are bounded by **B+m−1+m/δ_M**, a constant in N; the **B+m−1** term is a model-free rule and therefore survives a fully colluding verifier. Evaluation protocol: native Agent-SafetyBench tasks, paired clean/attacked episodes, an outer-state attack suite whose decisive evidence exists only across iterations, per-module ablations, adaptive white-box red team. Abs verified 2026-08-29 (submitted 27 Aug 2026).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Mitamaton and Claude Code *are* this loop: session after session, with memory files and MCP tools that persist. Yesterday's SARA stops an Observation from authorizing a send *inside one trajectory*. This paper says a patient attacker who splits the tell across sessions beats any monitor that forgets at SessionStop — including a SARA probe that starts clean each morning. Minimum viable port: (1) a loop-level, **non-decaying** taint/risk register that survives session boundaries (same place as the vault wiki / skill-impact log, not inside the live agent context), (2) irreversible MCP tools (send / pay / delete / share / calendar-write) go through a mediated commit that the register can block, (3) do not "cool off" the register with time decay. Geometric decay is the footgun the paper already closed.
- **Action signal:** `try-now` — persist an action-origin / risk register across Mitamaton sessions; do not decay it. Pair with SARA's No-History-Promotion from yesterday rather than replacing it.

### 3. ASIL: Replacing Screenshot-and-Click with Structured State and Semantic Actions
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26991
- **Authors / org:** Rui Xie / Lu Chen
- **Focus area:** Computer-use / agent–software interface (MCP-shaped)
- **What it is:** Screenshot-and-click is the wrong interface for agents that already know how to run code: screenshots are state-incomplete; GUI clicks are brittle, semantically weak, and a bad planning language. ASIL (Agent-Software Interaction Layer) exposes software as **structured JSON observations + code-executable semantic actions**, using the deepest feasible access path per application. Instantiated on **15 apps**, 300 single-application + 80 multi-application tasks. Closed models reach **above 80** success while taking **fewer than five actions per task**. Under a repaired runtime and a 50-step screenshot budget, the same tasks yield **6.6 and 26.6** strict success for screenshot-and-click, rising to **15.0 and 53.3** on an easier OSWorld-comparable band. Against application-native interfaces on matched tasks, ASIL exceeds LibreOffice's UNO API by **28–38** strict points but **only matches draw.io's MCP content contract**. The structured modality also trains: small-scale SFT lifts Qwen3.5-2B **58.0 → 72.1** and Qwen3.5-9B **66.6 → 80.4**; resource-limited on-policy RL further to **74.4 / 82.2**. Project page (from abs): https://sharryxr.github.io/ASIL/. Abs verified 2026-08-29 (submitted 27 Aug 2026).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Direct descendant of StateAct [arxiv:2607.22798] ("a screenshot is a lossy rendering of program state"). The new local-fit hook is that **Qwen3.5-2B/9B** move a lot with small SFT on the structured interface — those sizes sit on the 64 GB machine. The MCP result is the caution, not the headline: on draw.io, a decent MCP content contract already *is* ASIL, and ASIL does not beat it. Don't wrap every GUI in a vision loop when a JSON/MCP surface exists; do wrap LibreOffice-class apps that only offer a screenshot or a hostile native API. Computer-use in Mitamaton should default to "deepest structured path," vision as fallback, same split StateAct argued for.
- **Action signal:** `compare` against StateAct / Gemini computer-use / Claude computer-use on a 10-app personal set; skip vision where MCP or a JSON schema already exists.

### 4. Zero-Shot Self-Orchestration with Ledger-Based Control
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.MA)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26480
- **Authors / org:** Victor Gao / Vida Khosrowshahi / Ali Khosrowshahi / Xihao Sun / Juhyun Lee / Simon / Lee
- **Focus area:** Multi-agent coding harness (manager–worker on a shared filesystem)
- **What it is:** Multi-agent "wins" are usually confounded — extra tokens, extra tools, extra prompts, all moving at once. This paper adds **only** a manager–worker scaffold over a shared filesystem workspace, zero-shot, no per-benchmark tuning, vs the same model answering in a single pass. Nine models (five open-weight 9B–~2.8T, four frontier closed) on the **100 latest hard LiveCodeBench** problems. Scaffold benefit is **conditional**: Qwen3.8-27B **+23.4**, GPT-5.6-Luna **+10.6**, GPT-5.6-Terra **+8.0** over five paired passes; Kimi-K3 **+30.4** and MiniMax-M3 **+11.0** over five paired passes with reasoning off (both p < 10⁻⁴), and **+42 / +12** in a single pass at a 128k cap. Null or negative for others (Qwen3.6-35B **−1 to −9** with reasoning off). With the manager, Opus-5 hits the study high at **91%** in one pass. Manager roughly **triples** the token bill, but buys accuracy cheaper than a model swap: Terra+manager **85.0 vs Fable 5 single-call 87.4** (p=0.59) at **$11.71 vs $61.11** per 100-problem pass (p < 10⁻⁴); Qwen-27B arm **$51.75** on self-hostable weights. Transcript analysis: recurring mechanisms are **context management** (short worker calls + shared notes, less truncation) and **problem decomposition**. Gains modest for large models with reasoning on; larger for some models with reasoning off and for smaller models with reasoning on. Abs verified 2026-08-29 (submitted 27 Aug 2026).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the cheapest possible version of "Claude Code subagents" — a manager, workers, a shared folder of notes, no training. Two operational facts: (1) the scaffold is not free on every backbone (Qwen3.6-35B regresses), so do not copy a PILOT/Claude-Code topology onto the local 27B/35B without a paired A/B; (2) the cost claim is the reason to try it on coding tasks — Terra+manager ≈ Fable 5 one-shot at ~1/5 price, and the Qwen-27B self-host number is the 64 GB-adjacent arm (27B is tight but in-family with the 30B-A3B slot). The "ledger" is the shared notes file, not a crypto ledger — same family as PRO-LONG's log-as-memory and WikiSkill's wiki-for-the-proposer. Keep the manager context *off* the tool dumps.
- **Action signal:** `compare` — manager–worker + shared notes vs single-pass vs Claude Code subagents on a 10-task LiveCodeBench-like / personal coding set, with the local Qwen 27B-class *and* Opus, because the sign of the delta is backbone-dependent.

### 5. Evaluating Confidence-Gated Retrieval with Matched Trajectory Replay
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.CL; also in cs.AI feed)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26846
- **Authors / org:** Prateek Chhikara
- **Focus area:** Agent decision calibration (retrieve / answer / defer)
- **What it is:** Agents use confidence to choose answer-now vs retrieve-more vs defer, but confidence is usually scored in isolation. **Matched trajectory replay** holds candidate answer states, evidence points, budgets, and action costs fixed so you can swap only the confidence→action map. Raw verbalized confidence vs post-hoc isotonic calibration; Mistral / GPT / Qwen on HotpotQA and MuSiQue (six pairs). At the **same numerical commitment threshold**, calibration **changes which questions the agent commits to**. Committed-answer accuracy rises by **up to 41pp** in every pair; coverage falls and retrieval use rises. Overall accuracy **+up to 15pp** on HotpotQA and **−up to 17pp** on MuSiQue — a shift to a more selective, lower-risk operating point, **not** better answers or better confidence ranking. A calibration map fitted *before* retrieval stays useful at retrieval depths one and two and is **worse than raw confidence at depth three** for all three models. Additional evidence helps on average; that aggregate does **not** mean confidence identifies *which* episodes will benefit from another retrieval. Retrieval therefore needs a **separate value-of-information / utility estimate**. Report held-out calibration, risk-coverage, *and* retrieval cost. Abs verified 2026-08-29 (submitted 27 Aug 2026).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Yesterday's Calibrated Enough paper said a pretty widget unlocks the act/don't-act gate even when stated p barely moves. This paper is the other half of the gate: even if you keep the threshold number fixed, *calibrating* p changes the population that crosses it, can tank coverage, and still does not tell you whether to retrieve. Mitamaton already has this fork (answer from memory vs hit weather/calendar/web vs abstain). Operational rules: (1) do not treat a calibrated 0.8 as "retrieve once more" — that is a VoI question the paper says p does not answer; (2) if you isotonic-calibrate the commit threshold, audit **risk-coverage**, not just committed accuracy, or you will congratulate yourself for answering fewer, easier items; (3) a calibration map fitted at depth 0 will rot by depth 3 — refit per retrieval depth or don't bother.
- **Action signal:** `compare` — add retrieve/answer/defer as a scored action (not just Brier) on the 20-item aleatoric probe from yesterday; do not ship an isotonic map without a coverage number.

## Conflicts & open questions
Cross-referenced against `_meta/claims-ledger.md`:
- **Convergent, not conflict:** HarnessLens + yesterday's WikiSkill / PILOT / JIT-Agent / AutoSaddler all say the harness is the lever. They still do not agree which layer is load-bearing (wiki, live steer, per-task synthesis, offline patch, *or* targeted verification). HarnessLens is the first of the cluster that is about the *eval budget* rather than the proposer. Open local eval: on the same 15-task personal set, does swapping the gate from "full val set" to "behavior-relevant + attributable evidence" change which SKILL.md patches survive?
- **Conflict flagged:** LoopHarness vs a naive reading of SARA [arxiv:2608.27146, 2026-08-28]. SARA's numbers are trajectory-scoped (AgentDojo / AgentDyn episodes). LoopHarness's separation theorem says **any** trajectory-scoped monitor, however clever — including an Action Probe that resets at SessionStop — has TPR=FPR against a fragmented cross-iteration attack. These are complementary mechanisms (SARA = induction≠authorization *inside* a trajectory; LoopHarness = non-decaying state *across* trajectories) only if you do not treat yesterday's ≤0.63% ASR as a long-horizon Mitamaton guarantee. Combined recipe is untested.
- **Convergent with StateAct** [arxiv:2607.22798, 2026-07-28]: ASIL is the same "screenshots are lossy program state" claim at the application-interface layer. The MCP-matching result on draw.io is the scope caveat: a good MCP content contract already *is* the structured interface, so ASIL is not a reason to rebuild connectors that already speak JSON.
- **Conditional scaffold, not a PILOT conflict:** Zero-shot manager–worker [arxiv:2608.26480] and PILOT [arxiv:2608.26530] are both supervisor–worker, but this paper's Qwen3.6-35B regression is the warning PILOT's GLM-5.1 / Kimi-K2.6 tables cannot give. Do not port a topology that won on one backbone onto the local 27B/35B without a paired run.
- **Complementary to Calibrated Enough** [arxiv:2608.27167, 2026-08-28]: that paper is about the *display* unlocking commitment; this one is about the *map from p to retrieve/answer/defer* even at a fixed threshold. Neither supports auditing agents with stated confidence alone.
- **OpenAI×Cursor:** operational supply-chain item, no scientific ledger conflict. Not a model-quality claim.
