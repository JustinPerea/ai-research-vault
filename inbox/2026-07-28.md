# AI Research Digest — Tuesday, July 28, 2026

## TL;DR
- **Anthropic ended the open-weights ambiguity** [anthropic-2026-07-27-position-open-weights] with a formal statement (Amodei, Jul 27) that models *without* dangerous capabilities are "a public good" and that the company has never advocated a ban — while endorsing three targeted measures (chip export controls, an industrial-scale-distillation crackdown, mandatory safety testing for *all* sufficiently capable models regardless of licensing). Notably still no commitment on Anthropic's own open-weight release plans. This directly reframes the frontier-lab consensus vault entries have been tracking under "how does the biggest closed lab talk about open weights."
- **Kimi K3 is out and it's open** [arxiv:2607.24653]. Moonshot released a **2.8T-parameter / 104B-active MoE with 896 routed experts (16 per token), 1M-token context**, native vision, and Delta Attention + Attention Residuals + Stable LatentMoE architectural tweaks. Kimi Team explicitly places it "trailing Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol but ahead of other open and proprietary models we evaluated." **Weights fully open-sourced**. This is the largest openly-released frontier model to date and the first credible open competitor to the top proprietary tier since DeepSeek-V4.
- **StateAct** [arxiv:2607.22798] — a **code-first, multi-agent computer-use harness** — pushes Claude Opus 4.8 on OSWorld 2.0 from 20.6% -> 26.9% binary and 54.8% -> 61.6% partial success at **~9x lower cost per task**, by delegating only 28 of 108 tasks (1.1% of main-agent steps) to a GUI subagent and letting the rest touch program state directly. This is a direct empirical case for **"screenshots are lossy renderings of program state; use code first"** — the single sharpest lever for long-horizon computer-use loops surfaced this month.
- **Two new agent-memory papers surfaced in the cs.AI new listings.** **SF-AMS** [arxiv:2607.22562] replaces static retrieval with a **utility-driven survival mechanism** scoring memory units by usage redundancy + temporal signals, adding **+9.65 F1 on multi-hop reasoning** with Qwen2.5-7B on LoCoMo/LongMemEval-s. **Temporal Context Reinstatement** [arxiv:2607.22575] is the mechanistic counterpart: long-context LLMs perform episodic-like ordered retrieval via a **single "time-reinstatement" attention head** encoding a one-dimensional temporal code. Together they cover the two ends of the memory stack — mechanism (why it works) and pipeline (how to prune).
- **The first formal MCP-vs-A2A empirical comparison** [arxiv:2607.23884] confirms what the vault has been reasoning about heuristically: **MCP is lightweight-implementation but requires application-layer handling of conversational state**; **A2A gives native stateful multi-turn coordination at higher implementation cost**. Narrow single-scenario report — no benchmark numbers — but it is the first attempt to fix the terminology.

### 1. Our position on open-weights models — Anthropic (Amodei)
- **Source:** Anthropic News
- **Link:** https://www.anthropic.com/news/position-open-weights-models
- **Authors / org:** Dario Amodei / Anthropic
- **Focus area:** Frontier models (policy)
- **What it is:** Formal Anthropic position statement, Jul 27, 2026, breaking prior silence. Core claim: "Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models" and models without dangerous capabilities are "a public good." Supports three targeted measures instead: (a) **chip export controls** ("we should not sell powerful chips or chipmaking equipment to China"), (b) crackdown on **industrial-scale distillation** by state-backed actors — "we're committed to cracking down on industrial-scale distillation through our own practices, including identifying and banning accounts" engaged in it, (c) **mandatory safety testing for all sufficiently capable models, open and closed**, on cyber / biological / alignment risks. Cites Anthropic's modular-training research + UK AI Security Institute report on irreversibility of open-weights risks + DOJ chip-smuggling cases + scaling laws. Notably silent on whether Anthropic itself plans to release open weights.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Two operational implications: (1) The "mandatory safety testing regardless of licensing" line signals Anthropic is willing to accept the same regulatory burden on its own closed models — reduces the risk that closed-vs-open becomes a regulatory wedge and Anthropic gets a moat. (2) The distillation-crackdown mechanism as described ("banning accounts") applies to **API-tier abuse**, not to legitimate teacher-student distillation. If you're building on Anthropic's API and use outputs to fine-tune a local model *for your own product* (not to build a competing frontier model), this policy explicitly does not target you — but the terms-of-service language around distillation may tighten, so check the ToS on next API renewal.
- **Action signal:** `track` — no code, no models, but a durable reference point for API/policy planning.

### 2. Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653
- **Authors / org:** Kimi Team (400+ contributors) / Moonshot AI
- **Focus area:** Frontier models (open-weight release)
- **What it is:** **2.8T-parameter Mixture-of-Experts with 104B activated parameters** — 896 routed experts, 16 active per token — plus native vision, 1M-token context. Architectural contributions: **Delta Attention + Attention Residuals** (improved information flow across layers) + **Stable LatentMoE** (routing regularization). Kimi Team reports "~2.5x improvement in overall scaling efficiency" and post-trains with RL across general / agentic / coding domains. Model self-positions "trailing Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol" but ahead of other open + evaluated proprietary models. Weights fully open-sourced. Submitted Jul 27, 2026. Full benchmark numbers not extracted from the abstract page — cross-check needed against Artificial Analysis, LMSYS, and SWE-Bench Pro when scores surface.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the **largest openly-released frontier-class model to date** and the first credible open sparring partner to Claude Opus 5 / Fable 5 / GPT-5.6 Sol since DeepSeek-V4. Two direct consequences: (a) **for local inference — 2.8T total is unreachable on M5 Max 64 GB** even at IQ1 quantization (>700 GB at 2-bit), so the M5 Max cannot run Kimi K3 locally; this is a *cloud-tier* open model, not a local-fit release. (b) **For agent design — 104B active MoE at frontier claim** means new serving providers (Together, Fireworks, DeepInfra) will host it within days, and pricing per-token will collapse below Opus 5's $5/$25/M rate. If Justin has any workflow using Opus 5 that isn't margin-sensitive to top-tier quality, Kimi K3 becomes the natural cost-comparison anchor once independent benchmarks land. Also — RL post-training over agentic + coding domains means it should show up in AA Coding Agent Index + WorkBuddy Bench cross-model leaderboards within the week.
- **Action signal:** `track` (or `compare` for cost-vs-quality comparisons against Opus 5)

### 3. StateAct: Program State, before Pixels, for Long-Horizon Computer-Use Agents
- **Source:** arXiv (HF Daily Papers #4, 38 upvotes)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22798
- **Authors / org:** Yang / Jian / Luo / Zhao / Dai / Shi / Yan / Liew / Savarese / Li (Salesforce AI Research inferred from author list)
- **Focus area:** Long-running / autonomous loops + agent orchestration + tools & connectors
- **What it is:** **Code-first, multi-agent harness** for computer-use agents. Central thesis: "a screenshot is only a lossy rendering of the underlying program state; code can inspect and modify that state directly." Design: main agent interacts with files, backends, and DOM elements via code (Python + JS); a **specialized GUI subagent handles only tasks that genuinely require perception**. On OSWorld 2.0 with Claude Opus 4.8: **binary success 20.6% -> 26.9%, partial success 54.8% -> 61.6%, ~9x lower cost per task** vs screenshot-only baseline. GUI subagent invoked for **28 of 108 tasks (1.1% of main-agent steps)**. A **code-only variant** (no GUI subagent at all) hit only 45.9% partial success — falling *below* the screenshot baseline — confirming the state-first + GUI-fallback split is doing the work, not either half alone.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the sharpest empirical answer to "how should an autonomous MCP loop actually interact with a machine" that has landed in months. Concrete design primitive: **any tool call that could be replaced by direct filesystem / DB / API access should be replaced** — reserve screenshots-and-clicks for irreducibly graphical work (visual verification, canvas apps, third-party UIs without APIs). The 9x cost delta is the actionable number: the same task budget buys 9x more attempts with a state-first harness. Composes directly with the vault's **Molt / OpenForgeRL** [arxiv:2607.21653, arxiv:2607.21557] agentic-RL trainers (2026-07-27 digest) — those give you the training infra; StateAct gives you the runtime primitive that's most worth training toward. Also composes with **NOOA** [arxiv:2607.20709] (2026-07-24 digest) "agent-as-Python-object" thesis — NOOA argues docstrings-are-prompts + methods-are-tools; StateAct argues the runtime that consumes those tools should reach *program state* directly, not screenshots of it.
- **Action signal:** `try-now` — the pattern is small enough to implement over a weekend against any MCP-driven Claude Code loop that currently uses computer-use / browser-use tools. Start by identifying which tool calls today are screenshots-around-a-known-API and replace them with direct calls.

### 4. From Proprietary to Open-Source: Multi-Agent Protocol Distillation in Agentic Search (MAPD)
- **Source:** arXiv (HF Daily Papers #2, 40 upvotes)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24280
- **Authors / org:** Junlin Liu, Jiangwang Chen, Zixin Song, Shuaiyu Zhou, Chunji Lv, Hank Wu, Kailin Jiang, Jinyang Wu, Bohan Yu, Chenxi Zhou (affiliations not extracted)
- **Focus area:** Agent orchestration + tools & connectors
- **What it is:** MAPD addresses the observation that **"conventional logit-matching is precluded by hidden logits and mismatched tokenizers"** — you cannot directly distill from a closed frontier model into an open student in the classic sense. Solution: an **offline multi-agent system decomposes queries, retrieves evidence, repairs searches, and converts traces into structured JSON protocols** containing task type + reasoning plans + grounding facts. This normalized protocol format becomes the training target for a privileged student policy branch, sidestyling both tokenizer mismatch and style drift. Evaluated on 7 QA benchmarks (names not disclosed in the abstract page). Results: **Qwen3-1.7B 39.4% avg success, Qwen3-4B 44.4% avg success**. Submitted Jul 27, 2026.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Names a **primitive worth naming** — "protocol-level distillation" as a third path between (a) API-level imitation (limited by cost) and (b) logit-level distillation (blocked by closed logits). If the pattern generalizes beyond search, it becomes a template for turning a Claude-Code-driven session into training data for a locally-runnable Qwen or Kimi student — the trace format is portable across tasks. Composes with **Experience Distillation** [arxiv:2607.21051] (2026-07-25 digest) and **ReOPD** [arxiv:2607.04763] (2026-07-25 digest) as the third answer to "how do you turn expensive teacher-model interaction into reusable offline signal" — ReOPD reuses *teacher* prefixes under student steps, Experience Distillation reuses *student's own* trajectories via ICL bootstrap, MAPD reuses *teacher outputs converted into a schema* so tokenizer mismatch stops mattering. That three-way split may deserve a vault primitive entry.
- **Action signal:** `track` — code doesn't appear released yet; watch for repo drop. Compare-worthy against Experience Distillation once the same benchmark set is measured under both methods.

### 5. SF-AMS: Strategic Forgetting for Structured Memory in LLM Agent
- **Source:** arXiv (surfaced in Jul 28 cs.AI new listing)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22562
- **Authors / org:** Ning Yang, Siqi Li, Miaoxin Shen, Yuan Zhou, Meng Zhang, Tong Li, Haijun Zhang (affiliations not extracted)
- **Focus area:** Agent memory
- **What it is:** Frames memory management for LLM agents as a **utility-driven survival problem**: memory units are scored by usage redundancy + temporal signals + a hierarchical structure prioritizing entity-consistent information over noise. Static retrieval (rank by similarity, take top-k) is replaced with an active eviction/consolidation cycle. Evaluated on LoCoMo + LongMemEval-s. Numerical results: **+9.65 F1 on multi-hop reasoning (Qwen2.5-7B)**, **+6.91 F1 on temporal reasoning (GPT-4o-mini)**, **+6.53 F1 on open-domain tasks** — all over the strongest baseline. Compared against LightMem, MemO, A-Mem (no direct Mem0 / LangMem / Zep comparison in the extracted abstract).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is a **memory-management primitive shaped exactly like the piece his stack is missing** — Obsidian-driven vaults grow monotonically and stale notes never get evicted, while Claude Code / MCP sessions have no persistent memory across compaction. SF-AMS names the *scoring* side (usage redundancy + temporal signal + entity-consistency prioritization) that could drive a nightly Obsidian curator pass. Composes with vault-tracked **AREX** [arxiv:2607.21461] (2026-07-24 ledger, dual-loop research agent that "compresses interaction history while auditing") and **mem0 / MemCon / LangMem** cluster as the missing scoring rule between "keep everything" (mem0 default) and "compress-while-auditing" (AREX). Also directly extends the vault's **PRO-LONG** [arxiv:2607.20064] "log-as-searchable-memory" thread — PRO-LONG assumes the log stays; SF-AMS gives you the criteria to prune it.
- **Action signal:** `track` — no immediate integration, but the eviction-score formula (usage redundancy + temporal decay + entity-consistency) is portable to Obsidian-side scripting.

### 6. Temporal Context Reinstatement Drives Episodic-Like Order Memory in Long-Context Language Models
- **Source:** arXiv (Jul 28 cs.AI new listing)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22575
- **Authors / org:** Mathis Pink, Vy Ai Vo, Qinyuan Wu, Jianing Mu, Javier Turek, Uri Hasson, Kenneth A. Norman, Sebastian Michelmann, Alexander Huth, Mariya Toneva (Princeton / MPI-SWS / Intel / UT Austin cluster)
- **Focus area:** Context engineering + agent memory (mechanistic)
- **What it is:** Mechanistic interpretability study of long-context LLMs on temporal-order-memory tasks over a full-length novel dataset. Central finding: **model performance relies on a one-dimensional temporal code reinstated during retrieval by a single "time-reinstatement" attention head**. The code operates like a timeline index — sequential position across extended text is encoded numerically inside the residual stream, and *one specific attention head* is responsible for reactivating that code when the model is asked "what happened after X." Aligns with human episodic-memory mechanisms in cognitive neuroscience.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Two operational implications: (a) **long-context episodic retrieval is not distributed magic — it's a single-head circuit**, which means it is both (i) more fragile than assumed (ablating the right head kills temporal reasoning) and (ii) more amenable to targeted intervention than assumed (you can steer or reinforce the head with sparse activations rather than retraining). (b) For memory architectures like **SF-AMS** (item #5) or **MemCon** — the temporal-signal component of an eviction score has a *mechanistic* justification now: models really do have an internal timeline representation, and a memory system aligned to that representation should preserve items in a way that keeps the head's activation coherent. Composes with the vault's **Temporal Context Reinstatement** thread as the neuroscience side of the "episodic memory in LLM agents" cluster.
- **Action signal:** `track` — no runnable artifact, but the "single-head" finding is directly citable when arguing for temporal-signal weight in memory-scoring functions.

### 7. Comparative Study of MCP and A2A for Multi-Agent Systems
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.MA)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23884
- **Authors / org:** Ionut Predoaia, Tuong Manh Vu, Konstantinos Barmpis, Dimitris Kolovos, Antonio García-Domínguez (University of York inferred from author cluster)
- **Focus area:** Tools & connectors (protocol infrastructure)
- **What it is:** First formal side-by-side implementation of the **same software-engineering task** under both **MCP** (Model Context Protocol) and **A2A** (Agent-to-Agent Protocol), evaluated against an industry-derived requirements set: discoverability, messaging, conversations, asynchronous communication, observability, interoperability, access control. Findings: **MCP supports coordination "through a comparatively lightweight implementation model with lower coordination complexity"** but **requires explicit application-layer handling of conversational state**; **A2A provides "richer native support for stateful, multi-turn coordination through protocol-level abstractions"** at correspondingly higher implementation cost. Explicit disclaimer: single-scenario empirical experience report, no latency/throughput/code-size numbers reported, no universal recommendation.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the first paper that puts an academic reference behind the MCP-vs-A2A trade-off the vault has been reasoning about heuristically for months. The concrete design implication: **if your agent topology is single-user, single-session, mostly stateless tool calls** (Claude Code driving an MCP toolbelt over a single conversation), MCP's lightweight model is the right choice and you should keep it. **If your topology involves persistent multi-agent conversations, long-running handoffs, or asynchronous coordination across sessions** (a Cowork-style dispatch or a supervisor-worker setup that runs for hours), A2A's built-in stateful coordination will pay back the higher scaffolding cost. The vault's earlier **ATWZ (Agent Team Work Zone)** [arxiv:2607.22917] (also Jul 24) fills the *missing state layer* for MCP-based teams via a filesystem convention — the two papers together sketch a "MCP + filesystem for state" vs "A2A native state" fork worth naming as a vault primitive.
- **Action signal:** `track` — narrow report but citation-worthy for design decisions.
