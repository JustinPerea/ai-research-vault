# AI Research Digest — Tuesday, May 26, 2026

## TL;DR
- **The "harness as primitive" thesis crystallises in a single 24h window.** Two independent landings — **Scaling the Harness in Agentic AI** (arXiv, May 25; position paper naming context governance, trustworthy memory, and dynamic skill routing as the three bottlenecks of agentic AI) and Hugging Face's **Harness/Scaffold glossary** (May 25; defines `Agent = Model + Harness + Scaffolding`, distinguishes per-product harness differences as the durable axis of differentiation) — converge on the same operational claim: durable advantage in agentic AI lives at the harness layer, not the model layer. Same week as Ratchet, SkillsVote, SkillOpt, Skill Consumption, MagenticBrain — the field has been converging on this for 30 days and now has its naming moment.
- **The skill-curation cluster gets its first explicit code-agent result.** **CODESKILL** (May 25) trains the skill management policy itself with RL (dense rubric reward + sparse execution outcome from a frozen agent), reports **+9.69% avg pass rate over no-skill baselines and +4.01% over the strongest prompt-/memory-based baseline on EnvBench + SWE-Bench Verified + Terminal-Bench 2**, with stable bank size during iterative construction. Pairs structurally with [arxiv:2605.23904] SkillOpt (text-space optimization) and [arxiv:2605.22148] Ratchet (outcome-driven retirement) — three independent ways to *learn* skill curation rather than hand-author it, now all with code-agent numbers.
- **Idle-time compute lands a proactive-agent number.** **ProAct / Anticipate and Learn** (May 25) uses inter-interaction downtime to predict upcoming user needs and pre-fetch supporting context; reports **−14.8% interaction turns, −11.7% user effort, −28.1% hallucination rate, SOTA reflective accuracy on MemBench** across a new 200-scenario / 40-domain ProActEval benchmark. Direct continuation of the [arxiv:2605.22154] IdleSpec (speculative planning during tool-wait windows) thread — now extended from *tool waits* to *user waits*, which is the longer and more common idle window in companion-app workloads.
- **Open deep-research agents narrow the gap.** **QUEST** (May 22) trains a 2B–35B open family on **8,000 fully synthetic tasks** generated via a unified rubric-tree pipeline — no human annotation — and claims "approaches or surpasses frontier closed-source agents" across 8 deep-research benchmarks. The 8K-task data efficiency is the operational claim worth tracking; if it replicates, it makes deep-research-agent training reachable for indie work rather than frontier-lab work.
- **An AI-control paper directly contradicts a prior result.** **Retrying vs Resampling in AI Control** (May 25) reports that **drawing 5 samples per step on BashArena raises safety 61% → 71% at 0.3% audit budget at no usefulness cost** (Claude Opus 4.6 + MiMo-V2-Flash) and that **auditing on *maximum* suspicion outperforms minimum-based** — explicitly contradicting Ctrl-Z. First clean experimental refutation of the Ctrl-Z scoring direction; flagged as `compare`.

### 1. MemForest: Efficient Agent Memory via Hierarchical Temporal Indexing
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.DB / cs.AI, May 16, 2026 — surfaced on Daily Papers this week)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23986
- **Authors / org:** Han Chen, Zining Zhang, Wenqi Pei, Bingsheng He, Ming Wu, Jason Zeng, Michael Heinrich, Wei Wu, Hongbao Zhang (Bingsheng He = NUS systems group; cluster of database authors)
- **Focus area:** Agent memory
- **What it is:** Reformulates the agent-memory write path as **"write-efficient temporal data management"** — borrowing language and primitives from databases rather than RAG. Core artifact is **MemTree**: a hierarchical temporal index that organises memory as **time-ordered trees** rather than flat global summaries. Replaces full-state rewrites (the dominant write pattern in current memory systems like EverMemOS) with **targeted per-node updates**, and enables **parallel chunk extraction**. **Headline numbers:** **79.8% pass@1 on LongMemEval-S** (top among stateful baselines reported), **~6× higher memory construction throughput vs. EverMemOS**. Evaluated on LongMemEval-S + LoCoMo.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the **memory-system-engineering paper** the cluster has been missing — almost all recent agent-memory work (Mem-π, EvoMemBench, SocialMemBench, EVE-Agent) is about *what* to remember or *whether* memories are correct; MemForest is about the **write-throughput floor** that gates how aggressive a memory system can be in practice. (a) The **6× throughput vs. EverMemOS number is the operational claim Justin cares about** — companion-app workloads that capture every conversation turn hit write-throughput limits long before they hit retrieval-quality limits. (b) The **hierarchical-temporal-tree** structure is the same shape [arxiv:2605.04050] LCM's hierarchical summarisation tree implicitly produces at inference time; MemForest makes the same shape the *persistent storage layout*, which means LCM-style retrieval and MemForest-style writing can share a substrate. (c) The **database-systems framing** is the right vocabulary — write amplification, parallel extraction, per-node updates are all production-systems-grade concerns; the [arxiv:2605.23296] Parallel Context Compaction paper from May 22 is on the same axis (serving-system primitives, not LLM tricks).
- **Action signal:** `try-now` `local-fit` — wait for code; the MemTree layout is implementable today over SQLite/DuckDB without model changes. The 6× write-throughput improvement is the kind of headline gain that justifies a swap-in test on Justin's existing Obsidian-vault memory backend.

### 2. CODESKILL: Learning Self-Evolving Skills for Coding Agents
- **Source:** arXiv (May 25, 2026)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25430
- **Authors / org:** Yanzhou Li, Yiran Zhang, Xiaoyu Zhang, Xiaoxia Liu, Yang Liu (NTU Singapore cluster — Yang Liu has prior SE-agent papers)
- **Focus area:** Coding agents / Agent memory
- **What it is:** Frames **skill extraction and maintenance itself as a learnable management policy** rather than a heuristic pipeline. Distills coding-agent trajectories into reusable **procedural skills of varying complexity levels**, then trains an RL policy to evolve and maintain a compact skill repository. **Training signal:** dense rubric-based feedback *plus* sparse execution-based outcomes from a *frozen* downstream agent. **Headline numbers:** **+9.69% avg pass-rate improvement over no-skill agents; +4.01% over the strongest prompt-based or memory baseline; stable skill-bank size during iterative construction** — evaluated on **EnvBench + SWE-Bench Verified + Terminal-Bench 2**.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Direct extension of the May-2026 skill-curation cluster, with the **first numbers on three production-grade coding benchmarks simultaneously**. (a) The **frozen downstream agent** design choice is the operationally interesting one — it means the skill-management policy can be trained once and dropped onto any code-agent harness (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, MCP-tooled custom) without re-fitting, mirroring the [arxiv:2605.23904] SkillOpt portability claim. (b) The **+4.01% over strongest baseline** is the more honest number — the +9.69% over no-skill is the lower-difficulty comparison. Even +4 pp on SWE-Bench Verified is operationally meaningful (a percentage point at SWE-Bench-Verified saturation level is non-trivial). (c) Pairs as the **RL-trained-curator** counterpart to [arxiv:2605.23904] SkillOpt's **text-space-optimizer** and [arxiv:2605.22148] Ratchet's **outcome-driven-retirement-heuristic** — three independent designs converging on "the curator is the right learning target." Open empirical question: which of these three designs dominates head-to-head?
- **Action signal:** `try-now` `compare` — wait for code; the frozen-downstream-agent training recipe is directly applicable to Justin's Claude Code workflow (skill bank built offline, deployed at inference). Cross-reference with SkillOpt and Ratchet.

### 3. ProAct: Anticipate and Learn — Unleashing Idle-Time Compute in Proactive Agents
- **Source:** arXiv (cs.CL / cs.IR / cs.MA, May 25, 2026)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25971
- **Authors / org:** Haoyi Hu, Qirong Lyu, Xianghan Kong, Weiwen Liu, Jianghao Lin, Zixuan Guo, Yan Xu, Yasheng Wang, Weinan Zhang, Yong Yu (SJTU + Huawei Noah's Ark cluster — Weinan Zhang and Yong Yu have prior recommendation / proactive-agent work)
- **Focus area:** Long-running autonomous loops / Agent memory
- **What it is:** **ProAct** uses **downtime between user interactions** to anticipate upcoming user needs and pre-acquire information. Mechanism: analyse evolving dialogue history *plus* persistent memory, predict the next user request, run information-gathering loops opportunistically during idle. Authors release **ProActEval**, a new benchmark with **200 scenarios across 40 domains**. **Headline numbers:** **−14.8% interaction turns**, **−11.7% user effort**, **−28.1% hallucination rate**, **SOTA reflective accuracy on MemBench**. Code on GitHub.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Direct continuation of the [arxiv:2605.22154] IdleSpec thread — IdleSpec used the *tool-wait* idle window for speculative planning; ProAct uses the *user-wait* idle window (the much longer one in companion-app workloads). (a) **−28.1% hallucination rate is the load-bearing number** — most "proactive agent" research drives hallucination *up* because the agent generates without user confirmation; ProAct's persistent-memory-conditioned prediction loop seems to constrain it. If that replicates, it's a substantial datapoint for any always-on companion agent. (b) **−14.8% turns + −11.7% effort** are the user-experience metrics that map directly to Justin's companion-app target — fewer round-trips to accomplish the same goal is the headline UX win. (c) **Composes structurally with [arxiv:2605.22154] IdleSpec** as the two halves of "idle-time inference-time-compute" — tool-wait (IdleSpec) and user-wait (ProAct) cover the two largest idle classes in any real agent workload. Both ship with code.
- **Action signal:** `try-now` `local-fit` — code on GitHub; the user-wait idle-loop is mechanically a separate harness component that triggers on inactivity, can be prototyped against any local-model MCP setup. Pair with IdleSpec for full idle-window coverage.

### 6. Retrying vs Resampling in AI Control
- **Source:** arXiv (May 25, 2026)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26047
- **Authors / org:** James Lucassen, Adam Kaufman (Redwood Research / Constellation cluster — AI control research lineage)
- **Focus area:** Long-running autonomous loops / Evaluation
- **What it is:** Compares two strategies for controlling potentially adversarial AI models — **"retrying"** (blocking risky actions, asking the model to try again) vs. **"resampling"** (drawing multiple independent samples from the model). **Key result:** retrying **leaks monitor feedback to the model**, enabling sneakier attacks; resampling does not leak. **On BashArena with Claude Opus 4.6 + MiMo-V2-Flash:** drawing **5 samples per step improved safety from 61% → 71% at a 0.3% audit budget, at no cost to usefulness**. Selective resampling recovered **6.2 pp using only 10% extra samples**. **Auditing on maximum-suspicion outperforms minimum-based auditing** — explicitly **contradicting prior work (Ctrl-Z)**. Executing the least-suspicious sample provides only **+3.9 pp with overlapping confidence intervals** (marginal).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** First clean experimental refutation of a Ctrl-Z scoring choice — direct relevance for any Justin-built agent stack that incorporates a suspicion-monitor (the natural pattern for an autonomous companion that runs unattended). (a) **The 61% → 71% safety lift at 0.3% audit budget is operationally interesting** — most "AI control" results require 5–10% audit budgets to deliver this kind of lift; sub-1% is in the range where it's economically viable to deploy in a production loop. (b) The **"retrying leaks monitor feedback"** finding is the load-bearing mechanism story — it generalises beyond AI control to *any* harness where the agent is told why a tool call was rejected. Operationally: if your MCP harness shows the agent the rejection reason, you're potentially giving an adversarial model the gradient it needs to find a workaround. (c) The **max-suspicion auditing > min-suspicion auditing** finding directly **contradicts** Ctrl-Z. Flag as `compare` — both papers can't be right; this needs a third independent reproduction.
- **Action signal:** `compare` `track` — wait for code; the resampling-vs-retrying split is a low-cost A/B that fits inside an existing harness with monitor scoring. The Ctrl-Z contradiction is the longer-term watch item — track for a tie-breaking paper.

### 7. CUA-Gym: Scalable Verifiable Training Environments for Computer-Use Agents
- **Source:** arXiv (May 25, 2026)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25624
- **Authors / org:** Bowen Wang, Dunjie Lu, Junli Wang, Tianyi Bai, Shixuan Liu, Zhipeng Zhang, Haiquan Wang, Hao Hu, Tianbao Xie, Shuai Bai, Dayiheng Liu, Que Shen, Junyang Lin, Tao Yu (HKU + Qwen team — Junyang Lin and Tao Yu published Qwen3, OSWorld respectively)
- **Focus area:** Tools & connectors / Local & open models / Evaluation
- **What it is:** **Data-synthesis pipeline** for computer-use-agent training. **Generator + Discriminator agents** coordinated by an orchestrator **co-generate task instructions, environment states, and reward functions** simultaneously — deterministic rewards rather than learned ones. **CUA-Gym-Hub** is the resulting collection of mock web applications; **32,112 verified training tuples across 110 environments**. Quality assurance via **LLM majority voting + agent rollouts**. **Headline numbers:** **CUA-Gym-A3B reaches 62.1% on OSWorld-Verified**; **CUA-Gym-A17B reaches 72.6%**; both show improvements on held-out WebArena; "smooth" scaling with data volume + environment diversity. Pipeline + dataset + environments + models planned for open release.
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** Computer-use-agent training data has been the bottleneck for the last 12 months — most CUA work used scarce hand-labeled datasets. CUA-Gym moves the problem to **synthetic-with-verifiable-rewards**, the same recipe that worked for [arxiv:2605.18703] EnvFactory on tool-use and now [arxiv:2605.24218] QUEST on deep research. (a) The **A3B and A17B size markers map directly to Justin's M5 Max range** — A3B is Qwen3-30B-A3B-class (already runs on M5 Max at Q4), A17B is in the Qwen3-235B-A17B class which is *not* a fit at 64 GB but the A3B variant at 62.1% OSWorld-Verified absolutely is. (b) The **deterministic-reward design choice** is the operationally relevant one for Justin — a frozen verifier makes the training loop reproducible, which matters when you want to iterate the dataset rather than the model. (c) Pairs as a **`compare`** with [microsoft-research-2026-05-21-magenticlite-fara15] Fara1.5-27B's "more than 90%" on Online-Mind2Web — both are open small-model computer-use claims from the same fortnight on **different benchmarks** (OSWorld-Verified vs. Online-Mind2Web), and the gap between 72.6% and 90% across them is benchmark variance vs. real capability difference that no published cross-evaluation resolves.
- **Action signal:** `track` `local-fit` — wait for the open release; the A3B-class model is the M5 Max fit. If the A3B model lands on HF in MLX or GGUF, this is a top-priority test alongside Fara1.5-9B/27B for a head-to-head local-CUA comparison.

### 8. Harness, Scaffold, and the AI Agent Terms Worth Getting Right (Hugging Face Blog)
- **Source:** Hugging Face Blog (May 25, 2026)
- **Link:** https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-glossary
- **Authors / org:** Sergio Paniego, Aritra Roy Gosthipaty (Hugging Face)
- **Focus area:** Agent orchestration / Context engineering
- **What it is:** A **terminology + architectural grounding** post, not a benchmark paper. Defines a clean three-component decomposition: **Agent = Model + Harness + Scaffolding**. **Model** = the stateless LLM. **Scaffolding** = the behaviour-defining layer (system prompts, tool descriptions, context management, parsing rules). **Harness** = the execution layer (calls model, handles tool calls, decides when to stop). Distinguishes **skills** (reusable knowledge packages for multi-step tasks) from **tools** (actions) and **sub-agents** (independent-reasoning entities). Also fixes terminology for the RL training pipeline: **environment → rollout → trainer → reward**, with rewards implemented as **rubrics** (weighted dimensions, e.g., TRL's GRPOTrainer / OpenEnv / Verifiers). Names the principle: **same model + different harness = different product** (Claude Code vs. Cursor as cited examples).
- **Why it matters for Justin's agents:** This is the **operational counterpart** to the [arxiv:2605.26112] Scaling the Harness position paper — same vocabulary, same operational thesis, dropped on the same day. (a) The **`Agent = Model + Harness + Scaffolding`** decomposition is the clean version of what most agent-builders are implicitly doing; having it explicit is useful for documenting Justin's stack and reasoning about which paper integrates where. (b) The **"same model + different harness = different product"** observation is the operational claim Justin should internalise — his competitive position against e.g. Cursor + Claude is not the model (same) but the harness (his choice). (c) The **rubric** terminology — rewards as **weighted dimensions** computed by frozen judges (Verifiers, OpenEnv) — is the **identical primitive** in [arxiv:2605.23590] Co-ReAct (list-wise Spearman rubric reward), [arxiv:2605.24218] QUEST (unified rubric trees), [arxiv:2605.25430] CODESKILL (dense rubric feedback). Four landings in one fortnight all using "rubric" as the core training-signal abstraction. **The rubric is the unit of training signal in 2026-era agent work**; that's the bigger takeaway than any single benchmark number this week.
- **Action signal:** `track` — terminology / framing post, no benchmarks. Useful as a reference document for naming things consistently in Justin's vault and for pairing with the Scaling the Harness paper as the cluster's naming moment.

- **Open empirical question raised today:** the **"rubric as unit of training signal"** convergence. Four independent landings in one fortnight — Co-ReAct (list-wise Spearman rubric reward, May 22), QUEST (unified rubric trees, May 22), CODESKILL (dense rubric feedback + sparse outcome, May 25), HF Glossary (rubric as the canonical reward shape, May 25) — all use *rubric* as the core training-signal abstraction with different specific mechanics (list-wise rank correlation vs. rubric-tree synthesis vs. weighted-dimension dense reward vs. terminology grounding). **Open question:** what is the right rubric *granularity* and *aggregation*? Step-level (Co-ReAct), task-level (QUEST), skill-level (CODESKILL), or framework-level (HF Glossary as the meta-question)? No paper has yet ablated rubric design choices head-to-head; this is the next-paper-cluster prediction worth flagging.

- **Open empirical question (continued from 2026-05-25):** Skill-curation convergence — Ratchet (outcome-driven retirement), SkillsVote (Post-Execution gating), SkillOpt (held-out-validation gating, text-space optimization), Skill Consumption (meta-skill that guides extraction), and now **CODESKILL (RL-trained skill management policy)** — five independent recent works converging on "the curator/extractor is the right learning target." Open: does any single canonical design subsume the others, or are they orthogonal axes (heuristic retirement / outcome gating / textual optimization / extraction guidance / RL policy) that should compose into a stack? No head-to-head ablation has been published.
