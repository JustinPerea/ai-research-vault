### [Agentic Evaluations at Scale, For Everybody — Nicholas Kang & Michael Aaron, Google DeepMind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ubwb6NzegyA) — AI Engineer (?min, category: ai-agents)
**TL;DR:** DeepMind's Kaggle team argues eval harnesses move scores by 22% on SWE-Bench Pro and pitches open community benchmarks plus a non-saturating PvP Game Arena. _(transcript unavailable — summary based on title + description only)_

**Key insights:**
- The 22% harness-driven swing on SWE-Bench Pro is the data point to internalize: when Ara's eval numbers shift, suspect harness/compaction settings before crediting model differences. Two "correct" runs of the same benchmark can both be useless.
- They are shipping (a) an open benchmark platform anyone can contribute to, (b) a PvP Game Arena where models play poker/chess for ELO that doesn't saturate, and (c) a standardized agent exam that pulled 500+ submissions in week one. - Story of a Turkish wastewater-treatment-plant engineer building a novel benchmark submission proves the community-eval model can produce real signal from non-traditional contributors — relevant to any Anthropic-portfolio pitch about democratizing AI research.
- Practical pattern: when comparing models or harness changes, hold the harness constant and the compaction constant — otherwise you're measuring the wrong thing.

**Tools mentioned:** SWE-Bench Pro, Google DeepMind, Kaggle, Game Arena (PvP)

### [Bounded Autonomy: Between Free Will and Determinism — Angus J. McLean, Oliver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t4359sKBu4w) — AI Engineer (?min, category: ai-agents)
**TL;DR:** Oliver's AI Director ships 4K creative assets a day for 200+ brands and argues stripped-down HTML pipelines beat complex agents by 100×. _(transcript unavailable — summary based on title + description only)_

**Key insights:**
- Direct counter to "more autonomy is better": a hand-rolled HTML pipeline beat a complex agent CV-generator by ~100×. Useful corrective when scoping any Ara feature — start by asking what determinism you can keep before adding agentic flex.
- Three operational rules he uses at production scale (4,000 assets/day): (1) replace internet access with curated documentation, (2) ask how little context you can use and still finish the task, (3) never automate a job you can't do yourself. All three are directly applicable to Familiars and Nebula Nodes pipelines.
- "Models are naturally verbose and so are the developers working with them" — the right framing for any agent code review. Cuts the right way against Justin's tendency to scope-creep agent features.
- Oliver runs assets you've "probably seen and had no idea were AI" — strong existence proof that the boring, scoped, low-autonomy version is what actually ships at scale. Worth citing when arguing against premature multi-agent architectures.

**Tools mentioned:** Oliver AI Director, HTML

**For /bookmark:** `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t4359sKBu4w`
